# Course content guide

The v0.1.0 content is an original starter set awaiting native-speaker review. It teaches phrase recognition and recall; it does not claim a complete proficiency level.

## Editing content

`src/content.js` contains shared concept rows with one phrase form in each language, followed by Pinyin, Hindi reading aids, and Arabic reading aids. IDs stay stable because learner review history references them. A concept aligns communicative intent, not a literal word-for-word translation.

Every concept must have English, Spanish, Mandarin, Hindi, and Arabic forms. Every lesson lists existing concept IDs. Changing a phrase substantially may require a new ID; correcting spelling or adding a note normally does not.

## Review rubric

- Is the meaning useful, natural, and suitable for a beginner?
- Which variety, region, politeness level, and gender does the phrase use?
- Does the source meaning align with the target phrase in context?
- Are script, punctuation, Pinyin tones, transliteration, and spacing accurate?
- Are common alternatives or important differences explained?
- Is any claim about grammar, culture, or pronunciation supported by context?
- Is the material original or licensed for redistribution?

Do not label a course as reviewed based on automated translation alone. Require an author and an independent qualified reviewer. Record review status and provenance as the content system grows. Avoid stereotyping people or treating one regional variety as the sole correct form.

## Current language policies

- English: everyday contemporary forms. Future accents and spelling alternatives must be labeled.
- Spanish: broadly useful forms with regional notes, rather than a claim that one dialect is universal.
- Mandarin: Standard Mandarin, simplified characters, tone-marked Pinyin. Some tone changes appear in reading aids.
- Hindi: Standard Hindi in Devanagari. Reading aids are approximate and should fade as script knowledge grows.
- Arabic: Modern Standard Arabic only. A future dialect course needs its own vocabulary, audio, metadata, and review.

## Known expansion needs

Add approved gender/politeness variants, contextual dialogue, more script teaching, real audio recordings with consent and a compatible license, and unseen transfer tasks. For audio contributions, include speaker variety, transcript, permission to redistribute, license, and recording provenance.
